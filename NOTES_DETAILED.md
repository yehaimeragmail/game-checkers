## Game Notes — as if nobody has ever played this game

Checkers is a jumping game on a board with dark and light squares, where round flat discs hop diagonally and jump over enemy discs to capture them, like frogs leaping over each other on a pond. One player plays Red discs, and the other player plays Black discs. Each player starts with 12 discs. Red moves first. The player who captures all of the enemy discs, or who blocks the enemy so completely that the enemy has no move left at all, wins the game. This version is called the Tavern Classic, so the board looks like warm tavern wood and brass, and the discs look like bakelite, turned wood, brass-ringed discs, ivory, or marble, depending on the disc style you choose. It is a calm game with no timer, no dice, and no luck at all, except for the choices you make, which is why grown-ups say checkers is easy to learn and hard to master.

When the game opens, you see a splash screen that says WAJIHA, then Checkers, then THE TAVERN CLASSIC, then Ready! The main menu shows the word Checkers, buttons for Share, Rate, PRO, Settings, and How to Play, and a Game Mode card. On that card you choose how to play. You can play against a Bot, or you can play 2 Players, which means two people sharing this phone and passing it back and forth. If you play against a Bot, you also choose which colour the Bot plays as, Red or Black, and the Bot skill: Easy, Medium, or Hard. Hard is a Pro feature, so without Pro it shows a lock and sends you to the Pro screen. You can also rename the players. The default names are Red and Black, but you can tap a name and type your own, for example Ruby for Red and Onyx for Black, which are also the names used in the game's own settings. You can also pick a Tavern Style, which is the board theme, and a Disc style, which is how the round pieces look.

To start, choose your mode, your Bot colour and skill if you play against a Bot, your names, and your board and disc styles, then find the play control on the menu and start the game. The board appears with 64 squares, but only the dark squares are used, and the discs sit only on dark squares. Red discs start on the dark squares at one end of the board, in three rows, and Black discs start on the dark squares at the other end, in three rows. The middle two rows are empty at the start, like a river between two banks. Above the board you will see whose turn it is, and during a Bot turn you will see a BOT label, so you always know whether you should tap or wait.

A normal move is beautifully simple, and you must learn it before you learn captures, because captures change everything. On your turn, tap one of your own discs. The game will light up the squares where that disc is allowed to go. Then tap one of the lit squares, and your disc slides diagonally forward by one square onto a dark square. Ordinary discs only move diagonally forward, towards the enemy side, never straight, never sideways, and never backwards. Red discs move towards Black's end, and Black discs move towards Red's end. That is why a disc that reaches the far end is so special, as you will see below. If you tap a square that is not lit, nothing happens, and your disc stays where it was, so a careless tap cannot spoil your game.

Captures are the exciting part, and they are forced, which is a word you must understand clearly. A capture happens when your disc stands diagonally next to an enemy disc, with an empty dark square directly beyond that enemy disc, in the same diagonal line. Your disc jumps over the enemy disc, lands on the empty square beyond it, and the enemy disc leaves the board. That enemy disc is gone for the rest of the game. It does not come back. Now the forced part: if you have any capture available on your turn, you must take a capture. You may not choose a quiet little move instead, even if the quiet move looks safer. The game will only light up capture moves when a capture exists, so you cannot accidentally dodge a capture by tapping somewhere else, because somewhere else will not be lit. If after your first jump you land in a position where you can jump again, you must keep jumping in the same turn, chaining jump after jump, until no more jumps are possible from that disc. That chain is called a multi-jump, and it is how one brave disc can capture two, three, or even four enemy discs in a single turn.

Crowning is the second big moment in checkers. When one of your ordinary discs reaches the very last row on the enemy side, it is crowned and becomes a king. In this game the disc changes to show its king marking, and from then on it is a king disc. A king is much stronger than an ordinary disc, because a king can move and capture diagonally in all four diagonal directions, forwards and backwards, while an ordinary disc can only move and capture forwards. One important detail from the game's own instructions: crowning ends the turn. That means if your disc reaches the far row in the middle of a jumping chain, the chain stops there, and your turn ends at once, even if the new king could jump again from its new square. You do not get to keep jumping with a freshly crowned king in the same turn. Remembering that rule saves many arguments between friends.

Winning and drawing work like this. You win in two ways. First, you win by capturing all of the enemy discs, so the enemy has nothing left on the board. Second, you win by blocking the enemy completely, so that on the enemy's turn there is no legal move and no capture available at all, even though the enemy may still have discs left. If neither of those happens, the game carries on. A draw can happen in a two-player game when one player offers a draw on their turn, using the Draw Offer control, and the other player taps Accept. If the other player taps Decline, the game carries on. The game's deeper rules also allow draws by a 40-move rule and by threefold repetition, which means the same board position happening three times, but in a casual family game you will most often meet a draw by agreement, or by playing until one side wins. When the game ends, the end screen shows the result, for example A Noble Draw for a draw, and offers Play Again and Main Menu. Play Again starts a fresh board. Main Menu returns to the menu.

Here is a worked example with Ruby playing Red and Onyx playing Black. Ruby moves first and slides a Red disc one square diagonally forward into the empty middle. Onyx answers with a Black disc sliding forward. A few moves later, Ruby has a Red disc standing diagonally next to a Black disc, with an empty dark square beyond the Black disc. Ruby taps her Red disc, then taps the empty square beyond, and her disc jumps over Onyx's Black disc, which leaves the board. Ruby's disc lands beyond, and from its new square it stands next to another Black disc with an empty square beyond that one too, so Ruby must jump again in the same turn, capturing a second Black disc. That is a multi-jump, and Ruby's turn only ends when no more jumps are left. Later Ruby slides a Red disc all the way to the far row, the game crowns it a king, and Ruby's turn ends at once. From then on that king can slide backwards as well as forwards, and it can capture backwards too, which makes it very dangerous for Onyx's remaining discs. If Ruby eventually captures Onyx's last Black disc, Ruby wins, even if Ruby still has many discs left.

If you make a wrong move, checkers is strict in a friendly way. If you tap a disc and then change your mind before tapping a destination, you can tap a different disc instead, because nothing has moved yet. If you tap a square that is not lit, nothing happens. If you tap a lit square and your disc moves or jumps, that move is played, and there is no Undo button on the game screen to take it back. A wrong move in checkers never costs you a life directly. It can cost you a disc, because if you move a disc into a square where the enemy can jump it next turn, the enemy will be forced to jump it, and you cannot stop them, because captures are forced. That is the painful lesson every checkers player learns: never leave a disc standing next to an enemy disc with an empty square behind it, unless you have a plan for what happens next. During a game, the pause panel offers Resume, Restart, and Quit to Menu. Resume carries on. Restart clears the board and starts fresh. Quit to Menu leaves the game. On the end screen, Play Again and Main Menu do the same fresh-start and leave jobs.

Sound and looks live in Settings. Settings has Sound with switches for Music and Sound effects, a Volume slider, Appearance with Theme and Disc style, and a link to Checkers PRO. Music is the tavern background tune. Sound effects are the clicks, slides, jumps, and crowning sounds. You can turn each one off separately, and the Volume slider makes everything quieter or louder. The Pro screen explains Free versus Pro: Free gives you the complete checkers game, Easy and Medium bots, renameable players, music and sound effects, some tavern themes and disc styles, while Pro adds the Hard bot, more tavern themes, and more disc styles. Your settings, names, theme, disc style, and Pro unlock are saved on your device, so they are still there when you reopen the game. To exit cleanly, pause, tap Quit to Menu, and leave the app with your phone's normal home control. Check in the game whether a half-finished checkers match resumes after you close the app completely, because the menu does not promise a Resume Game button for checkers, and a tester should record the honest behaviour rather than assuming a match waits overnight.

## What to Test — for TestFlight

Open the game and confirm the splash screen, the menu, the Game Mode card, and the Share, Rate, PRO, Settings, and How to Play buttons all work. Start a game against a Bot on Easy as Red, and confirm Red moves first, the board shows 12 Red and 12 Black discs only on dark squares, and the BOT label appears on the Bot's turn. Play a normal move: tap a disc, confirm only legal diagonal forward squares light up, tap one, and confirm the disc slides there. Tap an unlit square and confirm nothing moves. Then test a forced capture on purpose: set up or play until a capture is available, and confirm only capture squares are lit and a quiet move is not offered. Test a multi-jump: capture one disc, land beside another capturable disc, and confirm you must jump again in the same turn before the turn passes.

Test crowning: advance a disc to the far row and confirm it becomes a king, its turn ends at once even if another jump looks possible, and on its next turn it can move and capture backwards as well as forwards. Test a full win by capture: play a complete game until one side has no discs left, and confirm the win screen and the Play Again and Main Menu buttons work. Test a full win by blocking in a two-player game if you can arrange it, or note honestly if you could not arrange a block in testing. Test a draw: in a two-player game, tap Draw Offer on your turn, confirm the other player sees Accept and Decline, accept once and confirm A Noble Draw appears, and in another game decline and confirm play continues. Test a full loss against a Bot on Medium, and confirm the Bot captures, chains jumps, and crowns kings.

Test the wrong-move cases: tap a disc, then tap a different disc before moving, and confirm no move is played. Play a bad move on purpose that leaves a disc open to capture, and confirm the enemy is forced to capture it next turn, and that there is no Undo to save it. Test Restart from the pause panel and confirm the board resets to 12 discs per side. Test Quit to Menu and confirm you return to the menu. Test sound: turn Music off and confirm the tune stops but slide sounds remain, turn Sound effects off and confirm slide sounds stop, and move the Volume slider and confirm loudness changes. Test Bot skill: play Easy, then Medium, and confirm Medium plays more sharply, and confirm Hard is locked without Pro. Test disc style and theme changes in Settings and confirm the board actually changes. Test exit and reopen: close the app completely, reopen it, and confirm your names, theme, disc style, and sound choices are still there, and record honestly whether a half-finished match resumed, because that point is marked check in the game.
